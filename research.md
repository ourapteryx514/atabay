<div class="research-intro">
  <p>
    Professor Naz Mohammed Agh-Atabay's research spans coordination and
    organometallic chemistry, heterocyclic and macrocyclic compounds,
    ligand design, and biologically active metal complexes. His work
    combines synthetic chemistry with spectroscopic, structural and,
    in later studies, computational methods.
  </p>
</div>


<div class="research-area">

  <div class="research-area-number">01</div>

  <div class="research-area-content">

    <h2>Coordination & Organometallic Chemistry</h2>

    <p>
      A recurring theme of Professor Agh-Atabay's research has been the
      synthesis and characterization of coordination and organometallic
      compounds involving transition metals.
    </p>

    <p>
      His work has included studies of molybdenum and tungsten systems,
      as well as palladium, zinc, iron and other metal complexes. These
      investigations examined metal–ligand coordination, molecular
      structure and the influence of ligand architecture on the resulting
      complexes.
    </p>

    <div class="research-keywords">
      <span>Transition metals</span>
      <span>Metal complexes</span>
      <span>Ligand design</span>
      <span>Organometallic chemistry</span>
    </div>

  </div>

</div>


<div class="research-area">

  <div class="research-area-number">02</div>

  <div class="research-area-content">

    <h2>Benzimidazole Ligands & Metal Complexes</h2>

    <p>
      Benzimidazole-derived ligands constitute an important part of his
      research. A series of bis-benzimidazole and related chelating
      compounds were synthesized and investigated for their ability to
      coordinate transition-metal ions.
    </p>

    <p>
      This work included palladium(II), zinc(II), iron(III) and related
      complexes, with particular attention to coordination geometry,
      structural characterization and the chemical properties of the
      resulting compounds.
    </p>

    <div class="research-keywords">
      <span>Benzimidazoles</span>
      <span>Chelating ligands</span>
      <span>Palladium complexes</span>
      <span>Zinc complexes</span>
    </div>

  </div>

</div>


<div class="research-area">

  <div class="research-area-number">03</div>

  <div class="research-area-content">

    <h2>Macrocyclic & Heterocyclic Chemistry</h2>

    <p>
      Later research increasingly addressed the design and synthesis of
      macrocyclic and heterocyclic molecular systems, including
      amide- and imine-containing macrocycles and mixed aza, oxo and
      thia structures.
    </p>

    <p>
      These studies explored synthetic routes to new cyclic molecular
      architectures and investigated their structural and chemical
      properties. Macrocyclic chemistry also became an important subject
      of graduate research conducted under his supervision.
    </p>

    <div class="research-keywords">
      <span>Macrocycles</span>
      <span>Heterocycles</span>
      <span>Cyclophanes</span>
      <span>Amide & imine systems</span>
    </div>

  </div>

</div>


<div class="research-area">

  <div class="research-area-number">04</div>

  <div class="research-area-content">

    <h2>Bioactive Compounds</h2>

    <p>
      An important extension of the synthetic work was the investigation
      of biological activity. Newly prepared ligands, macrocyclic
      compounds and transition-metal complexes were evaluated for
      antimicrobial properties.
    </p>

    <p>
      This research connected coordination and synthetic chemistry with
      medicinally relevant questions, examining how molecular structure,
      ligand systems and metal coordination could influence biological
      activity.
    </p>

    <div class="research-keywords">
      <span>Antimicrobial activity</span>
      <span>Bioactive ligands</span>
      <span>Medicinal chemistry</span>
      <span>Structure–activity relationships</span>
    </div>

  </div>

</div>


<div class="research-area">

  <div class="research-area-number">05</div>

  <div class="research-area-content">

    <h2>Spectroscopic & Structural Characterization</h2>

    <p>
      Structural characterization has been central to Professor
      Agh-Atabay's research. Synthesized compounds were investigated
      using complementary analytical and spectroscopic techniques,
      including FT-IR, FT-Raman, proton and carbon NMR spectroscopy,
      mass spectrometry and other structural methods.
    </p>

    <p>
      Later work also incorporated computational approaches, including
      density-functional calculations, alongside experimental
      spectroscopy to investigate molecular and coordination structures.
    </p>

    <div class="research-keywords">
      <span>FT-IR</span>
      <span>FT-Raman</span>
      <span>NMR</span>
      <span>Mass spectrometry</span>
      <span>DFT</span>
    </div>

  </div>

</div>
