<div class="research-intro">

  <p>
    Professor Naz Mohammed Agh-Atabay's research spans inorganic,
    coordination and organometallic chemistry, molecular materials,
    ligand design, heterocyclic and macrocyclic chemistry,
    spectroscopy, computational chemistry and biologically active
    metal complexes.
  </p>

</div>


<div class="research-area">

  <div class="research-area-number">01</div>

  <div class="research-area-content">

    <h2>Coordination & Organometallic Chemistry</h2>

    <p>
      Transition-metal chemistry forms one of the longest-running themes
      of Professor Agh-Atabay's research. His early work included
      molybdenum(VI), tungsten(VI), rhodium and platinum systems,
      followed by extensive research on molybdenum and tungsten
      organometallic complexes.
    </p>

    <p>
      Studies addressed metal-promoted alkyne chemistry,
      ethenethiolato and vinyl complexes, alkyne dimerisation and
      trimerisation, metallacycle formation and the structural
      chemistry of transition-metal compounds.
    </p>

    <div class="research-keywords">
      <span>Molybdenum</span>
      <span>Tungsten</span>
      <span>Rhodium</span>
      <span>Metallacycles</span>
      <span>Organometallic reactions</span>
    </div>

  </div>

</div>


<div class="research-area">

  <div class="research-area-number">02</div>

  <div class="research-area-content">

    <h2>Diacetylenes & Functional Molecular Materials</h2>

    <p>
      A significant part of the research programme investigated
      diacetylene-containing compounds and polydiacetylenes,
      including metal-containing systems.
    </p>

    <p>
      This work addressed synthesis and characterization,
      topochemical polymerisation, spectroscopic properties and
      optical behaviour, connecting synthetic chemistry with
      functional molecular materials and nonlinear-optical interests.
    </p>

    <div class="research-keywords">
      <span>Diacetylenes</span>
      <span>Polydiacetylenes</span>
      <span>Molecular materials</span>
      <span>Optical properties</span>
      <span>Polymerisation</span>
    </div>

  </div>

</div>


<div class="research-area">

  <div class="research-area-number">03</div>

  <div class="research-area-content">

    <h2>Benzimidazole Ligands & Transition-Metal Complexes</h2>

    <p>
      Benzimidazole-derived chelating ligands became a major theme
      of Professor Agh-Atabay's later research. New bis-benzimidazole,
      thioether and multidentate ligand systems were synthesized and
      coordinated with transition-metal ions.
    </p>

    <p>
      The resulting palladium, zinc, iron, cadmium, mercury and related
      complexes were studied through structural and spectroscopic
      characterization and, in many cases, biological evaluation.
    </p>

    <div class="research-keywords">
      <span>Benzimidazoles</span>
      <span>Chelating ligands</span>
      <span>Palladium</span>
      <span>Zinc</span>
      <span>Iron</span>
    </div>

  </div>

</div>


<div class="research-area">

  <div class="research-area-number">04</div>

  <div class="research-area-content">

    <h2>Macrocyclic, Heterocyclic & Macromolecular Chemistry</h2>

    <p>
      The research programme expanded into macrocyclic,
      heterocyclic and macromolecular systems, including mixed
      aza-oxo-thia macrocycles, cyclophanes, dendritic molecules
      and multidentate macromolecular ligands.
    </p>

    <p>
      These themes also formed a substantial part of the graduate
      research conducted under Professor Agh-Atabay's supervision.
    </p>

    <div class="research-keywords">
      <span>Macrocycles</span>
      <span>Heterocycles</span>
      <span>Cyclophanes</span>
      <span>Dendrimers</span>
      <span>Macromolecular ligands</span>
    </div>

  </div>

</div>


<div class="research-area">

  <div class="research-area-number">05</div>

  <div class="research-area-content">

    <h2>Spectroscopy & Molecular Structure</h2>

    <p>
      Spectroscopic investigation has been central throughout the
      research programme. Techniques represented in the published
      work include NMR, FT-IR, FT-Raman and EPR spectroscopy,
      alongside crystallographic and other structural methods.
    </p>

    <p>
      Early work included specialized nuclear-magnetic-resonance
      studies of rhodium-containing organometallic systems. Later
      studies combined experimental vibrational spectroscopy with
      quantum-mechanical and density-functional calculations.
    </p>

    <div class="research-keywords">
      <span>NMR</span>
      <span>FT-IR</span>
      <span>FT-Raman</span>
      <span>EPR</span>
      <span>X-ray</span>
      <span>DFT</span>
    </div>

  </div>

</div>


<div class="research-area">

  <div class="research-area-number">06</div>

  <div class="research-area-content">

    <h2>Biologically Active Compounds</h2>

    <p>
      Later research connected synthetic and coordination chemistry
      with biological evaluation. Ligands, macrocyclic compounds
      and transition-metal complexes were investigated for
      antimicrobial properties.
    </p>

    <p>
      Selected research also addressed cytotoxicity and DNA-binding
      behaviour, extending structural chemistry toward medicinally
      relevant molecular systems.
    </p>

    <div class="research-keywords">
      <span>Antimicrobial activity</span>
      <span>Cytotoxicity</span>
      <span>DNA binding</span>
      <span>Bioactive ligands</span>
      <span>Medicinal chemistry</span>
    </div>

  </div>

</div>
