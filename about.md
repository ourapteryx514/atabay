<section class="about-intro">

  <p class="about-lead">
    Naz Mohammed Agh-Atabay is a retired professor of chemistry whose
    scientific work encompasses coordination and organometallic chemistry,
    molecular materials, ligand design, spectroscopy, macrocyclic
    compounds and biologically active metal complexes.
  </p>

  <p>
    His documented scientific output extends from the late 1970s into
    the 2010s and reflects an evolving research programme connecting
    inorganic and organometallic chemistry with molecular materials,
    structural characterization, computational chemistry and
    chemical biology.
  </p>

</section>


<section class="about-block">

  <div class="about-label">CAREER</div>

  <div class="about-content">

    <h2>Academic & Research Career</h2>

    <div class="career-timeline">


      <div class="career-item">

        <div class="career-period">1970s</div>

        <div class="career-details">

          <h3>University of Manchester</h3>

          <p class="career-location">
            Manchester, United Kingdom
          </p>

          <p>
            Agh-Atabay's earliest presently documented journal
            publication dates to 1979. Bibliographic affiliation
            records for this work associate N. Atabay with the
            University of Manchester.
          </p>

          <p>
            The research concerned dioxomolybdenum(VI) complexes
            of multidentate Schiff bases containing oxygen,
            nitrogen and sulphur donor atoms.
          </p>

        </div>

      </div>


      <div class="career-item">

        <div class="career-period">1980s–1990s</div>

        <div class="career-details">

          <h3>Organometallic Chemistry & Molecular Materials</h3>

          <p>
            Research during this period encompassed phosphorus–rhodium
            organometallic systems, molybdenum and tungsten chemistry,
            metallacycles, alkyne reactions, metal-containing
            diacetylenes and polydiacetylenes.
          </p>

          <p>
            Publications from this period document collaborations
            involving researchers at British universities, including
            Heriot-Watt University, and an extensive body of work
            with Jack L. Davidson and collaborators in organometallic
            molybdenum and tungsten chemistry.
          </p>

        </div>

      </div>


      <div class="career-item">

        <div class="career-period">1990s</div>

        <div class="career-details">

          <h3>Heriot-Watt University</h3>

          <p class="career-location">
            Edinburgh, United Kingdom
          </p>

          <p>
            Publications from the early 1990s explicitly associate
            Professor Agh-Atabay with the Department of Chemistry
            at Heriot-Watt University.
          </p>

          <p>
            Research included metal-containing polydiacetylenes,
            functionalized diynes and their structural and optical
            properties, with collaborations across chemistry
            and physics.
          </p>

        </div>

      </div>


      <div class="career-item">

        <div class="career-period">Later career</div>

        <div class="career-details">

          <h3>Fatih University</h3>

          <p class="career-location">
            Department of Chemistry · Faculty of Arts and Science<br>
            Istanbul, Türkiye
          </p>

          <p>
            Professor Agh-Atabay's later academic career was associated
            with Fatih University, where his activities included
            research, teaching, laboratory education, research-project
            management and graduate supervision.
          </p>

          <p>
            His research increasingly focused on benzimidazole-derived
            ligands, transition-metal complexes, macrocyclic and
            macromolecular systems, spectroscopy, computational
            chemistry and biological activity.
          </p>

        </div>

      </div>

    </div>

  </div>

</section>


<section class="about-block">

  <div class="about-label">RESEARCH</div>

  <div class="about-content">

    <h2>Evolution of the Research</h2>

    <div class="about-research-grid">

      <div class="about-research-item">
        <span>01</span>
        <h3>Transition-Metal Chemistry</h3>
        <p>
          Early research addressed molybdenum, tungsten, rhodium and
          platinum chemistry, Schiff-base complexes and organometallic
          systems.
        </p>
      </div>

      <div class="about-research-item">
        <span>02</span>
        <h3>Organometallic Reaction Chemistry</h3>
        <p>
          Studies of molybdenum and tungsten complexes included
          alkyne reactions, vinyl complexes, metallacycles and
          metal-promoted alkyne dimerisation and trimerisation.
        </p>
      </div>

      <div class="about-research-item">
        <span>03</span>
        <h3>Functional Molecular Materials</h3>
        <p>
          Work on diacetylenes and polydiacetylenes examined synthesis,
          structural properties and optical behaviour of
          metal-containing molecular materials.
        </p>
      </div>

      <div class="about-research-item">
        <span>04</span>
        <h3>Ligands & Macrocycles</h3>
        <p>
          Later work investigated benzimidazole ligands, macrocycles,
          heterocycles, dendritic molecules and multidentate
          macromolecular systems.
        </p>
      </div>

      <div class="about-research-item">
        <span>05</span>
        <h3>Spectroscopy & Computation</h3>
        <p>
          FT-IR, FT-Raman, NMR and EPR were complemented in later
          work by density-functional calculations and detailed
          vibrational assignments.
        </p>
      </div>

      <div class="about-research-item">
        <span>06</span>
        <h3>Biologically Active Complexes</h3>
        <p>
          Synthesized ligands and metal complexes were investigated
          for antimicrobial activity, cytotoxicity, DNA binding and
          related structure–activity behaviour.
        </p>
      </div>

    </div>

  </div>

</section>
